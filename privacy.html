Privacy

Short version: nothing you log ever leaves your browser, unless you choose to connect Google Drive sync. This page is about that one exception — what it can see, what it's for, and where your data actually ends up.

What this app touches

Drive sync asks for Google's drive.file scope, and nothing broader. That scope only lets Hours Ledger see files it created itself — never anything else already in your Drive, and never your photos, documents, or other apps' files.

What it's used for

Connecting Drive lets your own logged weeks — entries, categories, weekly verdicts, and close-out notes — sync between your own devices, so a week logged on your phone shows up on your laptop, and the other way around.

Where it's stored

Two places, both yours: your own Google Drive, in the one file this app creates there, and your own browser's local storage. There is no server behind Hours Ledger. Nothing under my control ever receives, stores, or is able to read this data — I have no access to it at all.

Connecting Drive also adds a few small bookkeeping fields to your locally stored entries and categories — which device last touched a record, and when — so that syncing between two devices can merge changes safely instead of guessing. These fields only get added once you actually connect; if you never press "Connect Drive," none of this exists in your data at all.

Sharing

Never shared, sold, or sent to any third party. Nothing is collected beyond what's needed to sync your own weeks between your own devices.

Disconnecting

You can disconnect Drive sync at any time from the app. Disconnecting only stops syncing — it never deletes anything already saved locally in your browser.
